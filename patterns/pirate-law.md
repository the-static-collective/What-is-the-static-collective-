# ☠️ PIRATE LAW — Hard Hull, Loose Rigging

Pirate Law is the Static Collective's portable distinction between **constraints that protect truth** and **conventions that merely shape practice**.

It began as a Haunted Toaster creative instinct: some things called "law" are better treated like pirate law — **more like guidelines**. The wider Collective needs that distinction because a system that preserves every accumulated convention as sacred will eventually calcify the creative field that discovered those conventions in the first place.

> **Hard hull. Loose rigging.**
>
> The things that keep the work truthful are law.
> The things that merely tell us how people usually build are pirate law.

Pirate Law is not permission to erase provenance, counterfeit authority, fabricate evidence, or destroy recoverability. It exists so those hard protections can remain hard **without turning every useful habit into a permanent commandment**.

---

## 1. HULL LAW — do not casually bend

Hull Law protects the conditions under which another person can still reconstruct what happened and who had authority to decide it.

Typical Hull Law protects things like:

- evidence and provenance;
- the distinction between fact, inference, proposal, invention, and acceptance;
- local human and project authority;
- deliberate human anchors;
- recoverability and meaningful receipts;
- unresolved states when the evidence does not decide;
- known-good states whose destruction would erase the ability to return.

A local project may have additional hard invariants because its domain requires them.

Those invariants remain **local unless separately demonstrated to travel**.

No project gets to universalize its own implementation merely because it works.

---

## 2. PIRATE LAW ☠️ — more like guidelines

Pirate Law covers conventions that may be useful, elegant, efficient, traditional, or widely practiced without being truth-preserving invariants.

Examples include:

- architecture and framework conventions;
- directory shapes;
- Git workflow preferences;
- naming conventions;
- UI idioms;
- development rituals;
- genre boundaries;
- how many branches are considered respectable;
- whether one project's mechanism is "supposed" to stay in that project;
- whether a toaster is allowed to become something much stranger than a toaster.

Pirate Law may be followed, bent, recombined, forked, ignored, or deliberately violated when doing so creates a useful experiment.

The requirement is not obedience.

The requirement is **trace**.

If a project deliberately departs from a known convention, name the departure when it matters and preserve enough context that another person can understand what changed and why.

---

## 3. OPEN SEA — nobody knows yet

Some territory is neither Hull Law nor established Pirate Law.

It is simply unresolved.

That includes:

- new mechanisms with too little evidence;
- contradictory specimens;
- speculative structures;
- one-off creative accidents;
- ideas that have not yet earned a stable name;
- tensions between two otherwise useful laws.

Do not regulate the Open Sea prematurely.

Explore it.

Leave traces.

Let repeated encounters reveal whether anything deserves promotion.

---

## THE PIRATE TEST

When a rule claims authority, ask:

> **If I break this rule, can I make reality harder to reconstruct or somebody's authority easier to counterfeit?**

If **yes**, the rule may be Hull Law and should be treated with corresponding care.

If **no**, hoist the flag.

This test does not prove that every rule with consequences is universal Hull Law. It identifies where a burden of proof exists.

A local project may still need a strict constraint for mechanical, safety, compatibility, or product reasons. That constraint should remain honestly scoped to the project unless evidence shows that the invariant travels.

---

## Local law stays local

A law discovered in one repository does not silently acquire jurisdiction over the Collective.

The default relation is:

```text
local discovery
    ↓
local law
    ↓
repeated independent usefulness
    ↓
portable pattern candidate
    ↓
pressure / counterexample / specimen
    ↓
possible Collective law
```

The direction is deliberately slow.

Universal hardness must be earned.

A reusable insight may travel long before its implementation does.

---

## Forking the law is lawful

A project may explicitly depart from a Collective convention.

A useful departure receipt is small:

```text
Convention: what the surrounding field usually does
Departure: what this project is doing instead
Reason: why the departure is useful here
Protected hull: what must remain true anyway
Witness: what will tell us whether the experiment worked
```

A declared fork is not automatically a failure of discipline.

It may be the experiment that discovers the next law.

---

## Promotion is evidence, not typography

A sentence does not become Hull Law because it is:

- written in uppercase;
- repeated often;
- old;
- aesthetically satisfying;
- present in many prompts;
- implemented in one successful repository;
- called a LAW.

Hardness should be earned by repeated evidence that violation destroys something the Collective actually intends to preserve: truth, attribution, agency, recoverability, or another explicitly constituted invariant.

Likewise, a former Hull Law may be narrowed if later evidence shows that its supposed universality was accidental.

Law may become more precise without becoming weaker.

---

## Relationship to Creative Field Laws

[Creative Field Laws](creative-field-laws.md) describe portable constraints discovered through creative work.

Pirate Law supplies a prior question:

**What kind of law is this, and how far does it actually have authority to travel?**

That keeps creative laws from becoming cages.

It also keeps creative freedom from weakening the structures that make the work trustworthy.

```text
trustworthy hull
      +
piratable convention
      +
unregulated open sea
      =
creative range without epistemic collapse
```

---

## Compact operating rule

When building, researching, composing, or wandering:

1. **Protect the hull.**
2. **Name local authority.**
3. **Preserve the trace.**
4. **Pirate the convention when useful.**
5. **Leave the Open Sea open long enough to learn from it.**

> **The law protects the record.**
>
> **The pirate protects the possibility.**

All aboard the riff raft.
