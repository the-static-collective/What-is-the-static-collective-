A plain graph carries vertices and pairwise edges.




> **THE GRAPH REMEMBERS ONLY THE HAND THAT FITS THROUGH ITS REPRESENTATION.**

General law:

> **DON'T QUOTIENT AWAY A DISTINCTION BEFORE PROVING IT SAFE TO FORGET.**

# 7. Positive ancestry is separate from distinctness

A system may prove:

```text
x != y => ID_x != ID_y
```

without proving:

```text
ID_y <- lawful_transform(ID_x)
```

So provenance requires two orthogonal questions:

```text
ANTI-COLLISION:
Are these falsely represented as the same object?

POSITIVE DERIVATION:
Did this descendant actually come from this attributable parent road?
```

Durable law:

> **DISTINCTNESS IS NOT ANCESTRY.**

Different hashes, names, timestamps, IDs, or digests do not by themselves establish parentage.

# 8. Presence is not participation

The band handoff asks each returning voice:

> **What happens to the song because you arrived?**

That supplies a useful participation discriminator:

```text
PRESENCE != PARTICIPATION
```

The key point is narrower: registration/presence alone must not be confused with an attributable participatory effect.
