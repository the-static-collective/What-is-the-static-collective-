# Ghost / Residual Presence — History Without Authority

**Status:** Portable pattern v0.1 — promoted from the Primitive Incubator on 2026-08-22.

## Pattern

A system may preserve consequential residue after a source, state, event, participant, or world is no longer directly present **without allowing that residue to impersonate current presence or acquire the authority of what produced it**.

Working compression:

> **History may remain influential without becoming sovereign.**

Or, in the older haunted vocabulary:

> **A ghost may witness that something was here. It may not pretend that thing is still here.**

## Why this graduated

The original incubator note proposed one deliberately small test: preserve a marked residual trace after a state transition that would normally erase visible presence, while keeping provenance to the event that created it.

That invariant has now survived translation across materially different local systems:

* **Full Measure — Collision Specimen 001** preserves refused and indeterminate `WorldEncounterResidue` as deterministic read-only influence while constituted state, reachability, and authority remain unchanged.
* **jublEchat — Residual Lineage v0** projects historical residue deterministically, remains `authority: none`, and fails closed when residue contradicts itself.
* **Haunted Phonograph — HAUNT v0.1** carries bounded encounter memory as `influence-only`; return residue may shape later proposal space without gaining score, timing, evidence, renderer, or execution authority.
* **Haunted Toaster** repeatedly uses residue as an expressive and compositional consequence: ghost topology, lingering atmosphere, damaged return, memory, and post-event state are valuable precisely because the aftermath is not collapsed back into the triggering event.

These implementations do **not** share one schema, runtime, ontology, or trigger. Their local meanings remain local. What survived is the smaller invariant.

## Portable law

```
prior presence / event / state
        ↓
attributable transition
        ↓
residual trace
        ↓
possible later influence / discovery / re-entry
```

Subject to:

```
residual trace != current presence
residual trace != current authority
influence != admission
memory != warrant
re-entry != original occurrence
resemblance != ancestry
```

## Required properties

A conforming local expression should preserve these distinctions:

1. **Attribution.** The residue can point back to the event, state, or encounter that produced it.
2. **Non-sovereignty.** The residue cannot silently gain the authority of its source.
3. **Temporal honesty.** Historical presence cannot be presented as current presence merely because the trace remains reachable.
4. **Typed influence.** If residue affects later behavior, that influence is explicitly bounded by the receiving system's local rules.
5. **Contradiction remains possible.** Residue may be incomplete, disputed, indeterminate, or mutually inconsistent without the pattern forcing narrative closure.
6. **Deletion is not required for forgetting.** Active influence may cool or disappear while historical evidence remains inspectable.
7. **Return is weaker than resurrection.** A trace may help locate, reconstruct, or revisit relation without claiming the prior world has been recreated.

## What this pattern does not require

This promotion does not establish:

* a universal `Ghost`, `Residue`, or memory schema;
* a shared residue database;
* automatic cross-project memory;
* a universal influence score;
* authority transfer through lineage, memory, similarity, or recurrence;
* identical refusal or retention semantics across projects.

Each project should keep its own receipt, storage, admission, and execution vocabulary.

## Pressure-test questions

When a system keeps a trace after something is gone, ask:

1. What exactly is absent now?
2. What trace remains?
3. Which event produced that trace?
4. What may the trace influence?
5. What can it never authorize merely by existing?
6. Can later evidence contradict it without rewriting history?
7. Can the trace cool, retire, or become irrelevant without being falsified or deleted?
8. If it supports re-entry, does the system distinguish re-entry from recurrence, reconstruction, or resurrection?

## Relation to nearby patterns

* **Stigmergic Field:** environmental traces can influence attention without becoming commands.
* **The Room Can Vanish; the World Remains:** durable relation can support meaningful re-entry after the original room disappears.
* **Crossing Discipline / No Unaccounted Consequence:** residue is itself a consequence and should have attributable causal ancestry.
* **Projection Is Witness:** a useful projection may testify about history without becoming the authority that created it.

## Residual fog

The pattern has graduated; several deeper questions have not:

* When does residue become strong enough evidence to count as a receipt?
* Which kinds of residue deserve durable storage versus ephemeral projection?
* When does remembered influence become too strong and begin functioning as hidden authority?
* Can a neutral cross-domain residue envelope exist without erasing important local semantics?
* How should contradictory residues be composed without falsely choosing one history?

Promotion preserves those questions rather than answering them.

> **Keep the aftermath. Keep its ancestry. Do not let the aftermath impersonate the thing that happened.**
