# Creative Field Laws

These are portable laws discovered through repeated creative-system work across the Static Collective. They are not implementation requirements for any one project. They are constraints intended to keep creative expansion from quietly destroying truth, provenance, or human agency.

Use this file as context before asking a collaborator or model to invent a new creative axis.

## 1. Weirdness can expand without reducing trust

Creative range and system reliability are not opposing goals.

A trustworthy kernel may remain narrow, deterministic, inspectable, and receipt-bearing while the creative field above it becomes stranger, broader, and more expressive. New weirdness should usually enter as an orthogonal primitive or composition axis rather than by weakening provenance, evidence, replayability, or resolution law.

**Test:** Can the output become dramatically stranger while the explanation of how it came to exist remains equally or more trustworthy?

## 2. Source is evidence, not a cage

Source material constrains what may truthfully be claimed about origin, timing, identity, or content. It does not require every creative decision to imitate the source literally.

Derive freely where derivation is lawful. Preserve the distinction between what was observed, what was inferred, and what was invented.

**Test:** Does the system know which parts came from evidence and which parts are creative freedom?

## 3. Garment describes what the world is made of; atmosphere describes what moves through it

Do not collapse independent visual or creative dimensions into giant presets.

A **garment** changes the apparent substance, surface, or material constitution of the world. An **atmosphere** introduces a field phenomenon moving through or around that world. Because they answer different questions, they should be composable.

Examples:

- photocopy + smoke
- grain + fireflies
- fracture + rain
- paper + dust

**Test:** Can either dimension change without requiring the other to change?

## 4. Unresolved is not permission to invent

Absence of certainty is a state, not a license.

If evidence cannot support a placement, attribution, timing, identity, or relation, preserve the unresolved state unless an explicit creative layer is authorized to invent. Prefer `unplaced`, `unknown`, `proposal`, or another truthful provisional state over fabricated certainty.

**Test:** When the evidence runs out, does the system stop claiming knowledge?

## 5. Converge produces a frontier child, not breeding

Convergence is not necessarily pairwise parent selection.

When a creative field already contains a population, state, or frontier, a convergence operation may lawfully produce one new child from the field's present tensions and evidence without forcing the user to choose two parents. The field is context; the child is the new proposal.

**Test:** Does convergence extend the frontier, or merely disguise a conventional two-parent breeding UI?

## 6. Preserve anchors; mutate lawful freedom

Human anchors and other high-confidence commitments define protected structure. Mutation should occur in the remaining lawful degrees of freedom rather than requiring the system to forget what is already known.

The stronger the anchor, the stronger the burden on mutation to route around it.

**Test:** Can the system become novel without casually erasing deliberate human evidence?

## 7. Coverage before randomness

A creative field should establish meaningful coverage of its available dimensions before repeatedly sampling the same familiar region.

Randomness is useful after the field has enough structure to make variation meaningful. Before then, randomness often produces accidental repetition while pretending to produce diversity.

**Test:** Is the system exploring the space, or merely rolling dice inside one corner of it?

---

## A compact handoff

Before adding a new creative primitive, ask:

1. What observed phenomenon are we responding to?
2. Which parts are evidence and which parts are creative freedom?
3. What invariant must not be weakened?
4. Is this genuinely a new axis, or a preset hiding several axes?
5. What existing primitive should it compose with?
6. What is the smallest executable experiment?
7. What specimen would prove that the primitive earned its place?

The creative field may expand indefinitely. The laws that preserve truth should not have to shrink for that expansion to happen.
