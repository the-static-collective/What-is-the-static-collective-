# Scrapbook

The scrapbook is a place for **active arrangement**.

Its governing rule is:

> **A scrapbook preserves adjacency without claiming equivalence.**

Sometimes several artifacts, metaphors, specimens, questions, or systems look more interesting when placed beside one another than when read separately. That juxtaposition may reveal a resonance, tension, missing distinction, possible traversal, or entirely new question.

The scrapbook exists to preserve those arrangements long enough to inspect them.

It is deliberately **non-authoritative**. A scrapbook page does not become a law because it is persuasive, a fact because it is memorable, or a project requirement because several things seem connected.

## What belongs here

- cross-project resonances that are not yet proven patterns
- clusters of artifacts that illuminate one another
- tensions that become visible only when several ideas are held together
- speculative bridges between domains
- conceptual maps and possible traversal routes
- arrangements whose value is primarily in what they make easier to notice

## What does not belong here

- settled implementation truth owned by a project
- evidence that belongs in `receipts/`
- human field-test records that belong in `specimens/`
- continuity breadcrumbs whose main purpose is future retrieval; those belong in `witness/`
- portable laws that have already earned promotion to `patterns/`
- stable shared definitions that belong in `glossary/`

## Scrapbook versus witness

`witness/` asks:

> What should survive so a future session can recover what was observed, unresolved, joked about, or inferred?

`scrapbook/` asks:

> What happens if we deliberately place these things near each other and look again?

Witness preserves continuity. Scrapbook performs arrangement.

A witness note can become an ingredient in many constellations without changing its epistemic status.

## Scrapbook versus pattern

A pattern claims that some portable structure has survived enough evidence to deserve reuse.

A constellation makes a weaker claim:

> These things looked meaningfully related from this position.

Promotion should require a reason stronger than recurrence alone: a specimen, a falsifiable test, repeated usefulness across contexts, or other evidence that justifies the stronger status.

## Constellations

The first scrapbook form is the [constellation](constellations/README.md): a bounded arrangement of ingredients, resonances, tensions, possible traversals, illumination, and residual fog.

A constellation should be cheap to make and cheap to abandon.

The point is not to fill the sky with permanent lines. The point is to notice which lines remain useful after we stop drawing them.
