# Memory Is Terrain

**Status:** Constellation / exploratory

## Ingredients

- [Field Traversal & Illumination](../../patterns/field-traversal-and-illumination.md): fields have position-relative visibility, traversal, and frontier.
- [Witness / Unresolved](../../witness/unresolved.md): stored breadcrumbs are not automatically active context.
- [Space → Sequence → Story](space-sequence-story.md): stable places and meaningful routes may assist reconstruction.
- Ordinary repository storage: a file can exist without being salient, nearby, or retrieved.

## Resonances

The usual storage metaphor asks:

> Is the information present?

Terrain asks several different questions:

```text
Does it exist?
Can I reach it from here?
Can I see it from here?
Will I notice it if I pass nearby?
Do I know how I arrived here?
```

That suggests at least four separable properties:

```text
existence
reachability
visibility
salience
```

Authority is separate again.

A file can exist and remain unreachable from the present route. A concept can become visible without becoming important. A salient idea can still be wrong. A frequently traversed region can still lack authority.

## Tensions

Terrain metaphors can accidentally make knowledge look more objective or continuous than it is. Conceptual space may have multiple incompatible coordinate systems depending on the observer, task, language, or history of traversal.

A useful epistemic terrain therefore cannot pretend there is one God's-eye map available to every participant.

## Illumination

The strongest distinction made visible here is:

> **Remembered is not the same as stored.**

For a working intelligence, memory may depend as much on what routes can be reconstructed from the present position as on what artifacts persist somewhere in the corpus.

This also reframes forgetting. Some forgetting may be deletion. Some may simply be loss of route, visibility, or salience.

## Residual fog

- What determines conceptual distance?
- Can two observers share artifacts while inhabiting materially different terrains?
- How should routes decay, strengthen, or remain historical over time?
- Is a repeatedly traversed edge genuinely stronger, or merely more familiar?
- When should an old route be preserved even after a better route appears?

## Promotion routes

A stronger terrain model would need a specimen where the same stored corpus yields measurably different reconstruction depending on preserved route, field position, or visibility state.

Until then, terrain remains a useful arrangement of distinctions rather than a claim that cognition literally has geometric coordinates.
