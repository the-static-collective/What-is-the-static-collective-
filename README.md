# The Front Room

> **The Static Collective is a public creative and research laboratory.**
>
> We make songs, software, research instruments, playable worlds, community tools, and strangely named machines — in public — while testing one recurring question:
>
> **How can something genuinely change without losing where it came from?**

If you just landed here from the GitHub profile, you are in the right place.

You do **not** need to understand the vocabulary, architecture, history, or repository map first.

## The shortest explanation

The most visible thing we make is music. Around it grew software, research, community tools, live-media systems, narrative experiments, mathematics, and a large public fossil record of how the work changed.

The common discipline is **attributable transformation**.

A useful crossing should let you answer:

1. What was the original particular?
2. What changed?
3. What relation survived the change?
4. What evidence or receipt lets another person inspect that claim?
5. Who still has authority to decide what happens next?

That is why you will repeatedly see distinctions like:

```text
fact           != inference
proposal       != acceptance
representation != occurrence
compatibility  != authority
public         != canonical
```

The weird names are intentional history. The job now is not to rename everything into sameness.

**Keep the weird particulars. Build better apertures between them.**

> **Orientation is not retrieval.**
>

>
> When many nearby doors are cheap to probe, inspect their boundary first: destination, relation, reachability, provenance, and relevance. Load deeper content only when the current question makes the crossing useful.

## The rule of entry

When a conversation or investigation begins here:

1. **Orient.** Notice the nearby fields and visible frontier.
2. **Listen to the current request.** The present question determines which door, if any, matters.
3. **Traverse narrowly.** Read only enough neighboring context to make the work intelligible.
4. **Keep authority where it belongs.** Current evidence and project-owned canonical sources outrank this room.
5. **Preserve fog.** Do not silently resolve missing, contested, or unknown material.
6. **Leave when irrelevant.** If the work has nothing to do with the Collective, do not force it through this house.

## A room is not the world

The Front Room is a stable landmark. What lives behind its doors may change.

That is intentional.

```text
stable landmark
    ↓
relevant door
    ↓
bounded traversal
    ↓
encounter
    ↓
new illumination
    ↓
changed world
```

The room should remain relatively boring while the Collective behind it becomes stranger.

## What to carry through a door

A traversal should carry only what helps reconstruct the neighboring field:

- why this door became relevant;
- which source or artifact is being entered;
- what is known versus inferred;
- what remains unresolved;
- where authority actually lives.

It should **not** require reproducing private reasoning or pretending the route proves the destination true.

## Minimal traversal receipt

```text
Entered: Front Room
Prompt: what made this door relevant?
Traversed: room → field → neighboring field
Illuminated: what became newly visible?
Evidence: what sources supported the traversal?
Residual fog: what remains unresolved?
Authority: where does the canonical claim live?
```

## Conversation entry instruction

For humans who want new AI conversations to use this room as orientation:

> **At the beginning of a new conversation, when GitBook is available, enter the Static Collective Front Room first. Use it only to orient yourself: establish the nearby fields, important unresolved frontiers, and useful doors into deeper context. Orientation is not retrieval. Traverse outward only when the user's request makes a neighboring field relevant. Current evidence and project-owned canonical sources outrank the room. Preserve provenance, uncertainty, and unresolved fog, and do not force unrelated conversations into Static Collective context.**

## Failure conditions

This experiment is failing if the Front Room causes any of these:

- every conversation loads the same large context bundle;
- nearby ideas become assumed equivalent merely because they share a room;
- GitBook notes outrank current project evidence;
- unresolved material gets completed for narrative neatness;
- unrelated questions are bent toward Static Collective concerns;
- the room grows until it becomes another database index.

If that happens, make the room smaller.
