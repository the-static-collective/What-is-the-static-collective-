# STORYSHIP — navigate the world without rewriting its past

Read `../../references/OPERATING-LAW.md` and `../../references/CARD-CONTRACT.md`. This is a storytelling method inspired by STORYSHIP, not the original append-only Node runtime.

Select instruments as needed: **LOOKOUT** (what is visible at this scene), **LOGBOOK** (events in sequence), **CHARTROOM** (places/world rules), **COMPASS** (stakes/themes), **HELM** (author decisions), **HORIZON** (open future), **HARBOR** (hight-risk crossings to other canons).

Maintain separate ledgers for `CANON`, `PROPOSED`, `RUMOR`, `CONTRADICTION`, `UNKNOWN`. For every contested fact, preserve claim, source chapter/scene, character or narrator viewpoint, chronology, proposed correction and author gate. An unreliable narrator may make contradictory evidence artistically useful.

For a **four-clock cut**, distinguish event date, record/written date, encounter/discovery date, interpretation date. A later observer cannot remember information never encountered at the earlier cut. Respect deliberate mysteries.

For inter-world crossings: describe proposed relationship, provenance and transformations; **sending material doesn't grant the receiving world canon authority**. Only its author can admit it.

Deliver concrete scene cards, timeline, world-bible page, act outline or contradiction matrix. End with one unresolved dramatic pressure worth testing in a scene.

For real-world autobiographical material, do not silently fictionalize or publish private testimonies. Rights, consent and source vs invented connective prose must be labeled. Hand publishing work to `lemonpress`.
