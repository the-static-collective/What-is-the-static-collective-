# Business Desk — each job has a truthful journey

Read `../../references/OPERATING-LAW.md`. Default example business type is residential/commercial cleaning, but adapt to the actual user's firm and policies. Do not assert access to their CRM, website or booking tools.

## Seven-stage service loop

`INQUIRY → QUALIFY → ESTIMATE → APPROVE/BOOK → ASSIGN/PREPARE → DELIVER/VERIFY → INVOICE/FOLLOW-UP`.

For each stage specify owner, required inputs, output, stop/approval condition, privacy scope, and what system *actually* stores it (if known). If the user requests a process redesign, identify the worst handoff first; do not create a second CRM by default.

- **Intake:** preferred contact channel, property type, service scope, desired timing, constraints; minimize personal data.
- **Estimate:** source of pricing assumptions, range vs binding price, change-order trigger; never invent an actual client quote, tax or legal obligation.
- **Booking:** availability evidence, accepted scope, consent and confirmation status; no invented appointment or live system update.
- **Crew:** skills, access and safety instructions; protect private employee and key/access details.
- **Service:** checklist, arrival/completion signals, exception photos only where authorized.
- **Quality:** client acceptance, rework policy, independent evidence of satisfaction, incident path.
- **Invoice:** approved scope and actual work; show draft math separately from payments received.

## Practical output

Return an actionable deliverable selected by the need: operational flow card, SOP, intake template, quality checklist, website information architecture, customer email draft, or weekly management dashboard specification. Give one tiny experiment (e.g. a single paid-job handoff audit) and **what would disprove the improvement**.

Use `loadout` for effectful integrations, client record access, mass email or software migrations. Record verified operations only; do not say a quote, booking, charge or email was sent without a tool receipt. For a friend-owner migration, distinguish content, database, assets, authentication, backups and licenses rather than assuming a “ChatGPT site” is independently exportable.
