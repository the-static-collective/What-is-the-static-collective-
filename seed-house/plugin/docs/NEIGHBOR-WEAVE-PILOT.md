## Pilot: one conversation, not a campaign

1. **Owner readiness gate.** Colton confirms whether he wants help, where Willow actually serves, permitted cleaning categories, insured capability, lead time, and whether existing WMS relationships already cover the need. Until then, cards are proposals only.
2. **Choose one lead.** Reopen its official source and verify it remains current. Use one source-grounded card; compare against a plain handwritten introduction.
3. **Construct reciprocal value.** Write both potential benefits and the objection most likely to defeat the fit. For example, do they already handle final cleaning themselves?
4. **Permission gate.** Colton chooses whether to approach. Draft an optional single individualized note; leave **DRAFT — NOT SENT**. Any real contact requires an authorized sending action and a separate human-verifiable receipt.
5. **Observe.** If the other party welcomes a trial, confirm its specific boundaries and retain dated reciprocal consent. Credit/attribution to a business requires its explicit approval.
6. **Return.** Record one of: useful two-way opportunity; declined/no-fit; unresolved; or useful collaboration observed. Compare labor/time with a simple paper note. Preserve refusal without repeated nudges.

## Acceptance and falsifier

**Success:** one authentic, voluntary two-way introduction, or a faster well-sourced NO_FIT decision, without duplicating WMS records. **Falsifier:** extra administrative work, confusing unverified claims with existing relationships, spam, customer-data movement, or a simple spreadsheet performing the same task more cheaply. If falsified, keep the one-page cards and drop the added apparatus.

## Boundaries

No invented revenue or cleaning demand; no scraping at scale; no bulk outreach; no publication of private personal details; no exchange of client lists, referral fees, pricing coordination, or exclusivity without separate owner/legal review. This is source-backed discovery plus a manual workflow, not an authenticated referral platform.
