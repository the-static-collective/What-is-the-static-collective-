# THE FIRST DOOR — A Room Remembers Differently

**Status:** Proposed creative specimen · not an admitted fictional event, screenplay, MEMENTO crossing, or project integration · 2026-09-23.  
**Parent:** [CANNON 001 — The House Takes Attendance](cannon-001-the-house-takes-attendance.md).  
**Existing event not amended:** [CANON 001 — The Collection Event](canon-001-the-collection-event.md).

> **A memory can help someone find a door without proving that their interpretation of the memory is correct.**

## Which first door?

The Collection Event is an already established *fictional* first shared act. This specimen takes place **later**, as a possible first personal entry for a participant who was not present at that event. An audience may meet this character before seeing the Collection Event, but a later presentation does not reorder what happened.

Every protagonist may have a distinct first door. A shared world does not require identical discovery order, inherited knowledge, compulsory participation, or one omniscient narrator.

## The Novelist scene-control frame

- **Primary genre engine:** grounded character drama with absurd animal comedy; mystery is secondary. The local promise is to find out whether a missing animal can be helped before nightfall, not to decode the whole universe.
- **Viewpoint:** the substitute teacher. He knows his own attendance list, a recording he has heard, and what is currently in front of him. He does **not** know the full mechanics of Renji's roomspace or the Collection Event unless the scene independently shows what he learned.
- **Entry state:** one bowl remains untouched; a sound on a saved clip has been labeled “pipes” by someone else. The substitute has not independently established what caused the sound.
- **Pressure:** he can leave the pantry closed and call the animal absent, or inspect the room and risk the practical mess of making another exit available.
- **Turn:** a rhythm in the pantry resembles the recording, but his inspection, not the resemblance, reveals an overlooked passage.
- **Choice:** he moves the obstruction and sets down a bowl. He does not drag the animal across.
- **Consequence:** a new route is physically available, while the animal's decision remains open.
- **Reader exit:** the reader can name the local need, what the teacher checked, and what changed, but cannot yet claim that the two knocking sounds had the same source or that the house itself is sentient.

The scene is an *invented proposal*, not a claim that the canonical teacher has already visited the desert house. The setting and chronology are open to revision.

## Proposed scene — The Eighth Bowl

There were eight names on the substitute's attendance sheet and seven empty bowls.

The eighth bowl was full.

He had been trying not to call this an emergency because his experience with emergencies was that naming them made people arrive with forms.

He checked behind the refrigerator, beneath the porch steps, and inside a cabinet whose owner had written NOT A CABINET on the door. It was a cabinet. He wrote CABINET on his sheet and underlined it.

Then the pantry knocked three times.

He had heard that rhythm before, in a saved clip from the animal detective's livestream. On the clip someone had typed PIPES in the chat. He had believed them at the time. It had been a reasonable thing to believe about a noise inside a wall.

He did not write RAT on his sheet. He wrote SOUND, NEW, PANTRY.

The pantry knocked again.

He opened it. The shelves were full of rice, a chipped blue cup, and approximately a lifetime supply of paper towels. At the back, a thin line of daylight showed between the floor and a wooden panel.

He moved the paper towels. The line widened. He put his palm against the panel; it shifted under its own weight, as if it had never been latched.

Beyond it was a short passage. At the far end, a small animal sat where the light stopped.

He could not tell from this distance whether it was the one whose name had not been answered.

*“Okay,”* he said. *“I found a possible door.”*

The animal did not move.

He set the eighth bowl just inside the passage, then backed away until he could see the opening without blocking it. The animal lifted its head toward the food. It might come. It might not.

He left the attendance mark blank.

A third knock came from somewhere farther along the passage.

The substitute looked at the list, drew a careful little question mark in the margin, and sat down beside the pantry with his back against the cabinet that was, in fact, a cabinet.

*“Come in,”* he said. *“We made too much.”*

The bowl remained where he had placed it. The opening remained open. No one had yet crossed.

## MEMENTO-inspired memory ledger — a design analogy, not a data import

| Layer | What this candidate scene shows | What it does **not** authorize |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source carrier | An older saved livestream clip carries three audible knocks and another viewer's “PIPES” comment. | The comment is not verified identification of the sound. |
| Earlier contact | The substitute heard the clip and provisionally accepted an ordinary explanation. | His earlier thought is not retroactive knowledge of a passage. |
| New contact / activation | He hears knocks in the pantry and recalls the clip. | A repeated pattern does not establish shared cause, origin, identity, or a door-opening command. |
| Inspectable occurrence | He opens the pantry, moves towels, touches a panel, and observes a passage. | Discovering this passage does not prove every other imagined passage exists. |
| Choice / consequence | He makes the route accessible, places a bowl, and waits without moving the animal. | Availability is not crossing; help offered is not help received. |
| Unresolved residue | There may be another animal farther inside; the eighth name is still unconfirmed. | No forced reveal or fabricated attendance status. |

If adapted into the actual MEMENTO workspace, an owning human/project gate would separately decide what source testimony, proposal, and local story fact to admit. This public seed neither writes into that repository nor creates a hidden ledger entry.

## Three independent entrances to the same proposed local occurrence

**Prose / film:** The substitute's attention is on a missing individual and an untouched bowl. The camera may show his attendance sheet and the unmoving animal. This version delivers a complete local dramatic change without explaining the livestream's origin.

**Sound / song:** The three knocks are a small rhythmic motif. The final knock may be heard only after the bowl is set down. The musical treatment must be marked as a new composition; it must not impersonate the exact sound recorded on the original fictional stream.

**Game / visual dream:** The participant can inspect the cup, towels, panel, and blank attendance mark. The opening is available after the obstruction is moved; the animal may wait or refuse. A later memory projection may show the old clip beside the pantry, but not render a shared cause as a settled fact. The player can end the scene without coercing the animal.

Each version should be intelligible by itself. Their differences are authored perspectives and transformations, not evidence that one medium secretly owns the other.

## Reader-model and continuity gates

1. **At entry:** the reader knows eight names and seven empty bowls; one individual is unaccounted for, not proven absent or endangered.
2. **At the clip recall:** the reader can infer why the teacher investigates, but cannot infer that he knows what produced either sound.
3. **At the moved panel:** the reader can point to the actual inspection that made the passage visible; the sound merely directed attention.
4. **At the bowl:** the reader sees the teacher's concrete help and the animal's available choice; the need is not falsely marked fulfilled.
5. **At exit:** the reader knows a possible door is open, with two mysteries intact: who or what made the farther knock, and whether the animal will cross.

**Hostile cut:** remove the saved livestream entirely. The scene must still make sense as a person hearing a noise, inspecting a pantry, and choosing to help an animal. Restore the clip: it adds resonance and a possible cross-media relation, not a missing explanation without which the film fails.

**Privacy / canon gate:** Do not treat real audience participation, collaborators, family, private memory, or actual aid requests as fictional source material without a separate authorized crossing. Do not amend the canonical Deuce/Renji/two-rat Collection Event to make this scene appear earlier or inevitable.
