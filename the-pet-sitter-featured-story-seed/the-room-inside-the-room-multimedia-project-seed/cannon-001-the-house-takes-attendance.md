# CANNON 001 — The House Takes Attendance

**Status:** Author-invited creative-universe seed · 2026-09-23 · proposed fictional material, not a retroactive rewrite of existing canon.  
**Neighborhood:** [The Room Inside the Room](./) · [CANON 001 — The Collection Event](canon-001-the-collection-event.md).  
**Scope:** _The Static Collective Movie_ and its possible crossings with _The Pet Sitter_, _The Room Inside the Room_, music, live shows, games, visual experiments, and real-world creative participation. The fictional layer is not a historical account of the real Collective.

> **CANON 001 is what we remember. CANNON 001 is what happens when we fire it into the world.**  
> Canon preserves what happened; Cannon makes room for what happens next.

## The one wild thing

The Static Collective is a fictional universe that keeps accidentally producing real things, and a real creative collective that keeps discovering fictional worlds inside the things it produces.

An impossible house in the desert is a narrative meeting place, not a literal headquarters or a universal software authority. Every song may be a door; every story may be a room; every witness may bring another piece of the composition. Nobody has to know the whole house before entering. Nobody gets to own the complete account merely by describing it.

**The house does not contain the whole universe. It only needs enough doors.**

## What CANON 001 already settles

_Do not retell the founding event differently to make this seed fit._

[CANON 001](canon-001-the-collection-event.md) places the *fictional* Collection Event in Deuce Burbank's nearly unwatched stream. Renji and a few later viewers contribute different limited glimpses. A provisional passage allows one rat to reach another; their choices remain their own. The stream, chat, diagrams, and later song provide attributable residue. The Collective **collects by helping**, not by discovering it was secretly a preassembled organization.

CANNON 001 begins *at or after* that shared act as an expandable narrative premise. It neither moves the original rescue into the desert house nor turns the house into the cause of the Collective's formation. The exact relationship between the house and Deuce's roomspace is **open**, not an established fact.

## Composition: six rooms, several independently valid entrances

### 1. The house — attendance without possession

A desert porch, orchard, kitchen, dog park, rehearsal room, telescope, and notoriously haunted toaster form a possible recurring story location. Some rooms seem to exceed the building's exterior; some may be memories, performances, camera frames, games, or speculative roomspace passages. Do not resolve their ontology merely because the imagery rhymes.

The house "takes attendance" by making arrival and absence *noticeable*. It does not assign belonging, compel participation, confer identity, or surveil everyone. The substitute's mundane attendance-sheet comedy may rhyme with this phrase, but the teacher remains the emotional center of his own film.

### 2. The recordings — same ingredients, different recipes

A song, Bandcamp sequence, YouTube edit, livestream, movie, game route, and performance can carry overlapping events in different orders, framings, attributions, and degrees of knowledge. None becomes the source-of-truth merely by being the longest cut or the newest release.

**Story possibility:** an abandoned demo appears to describe a place missing from another rendition. The missing place is a creative invitation, not automatic proof of a secretly true timeline. A remix can change the audience's route without altering a previously witnessed occurrence.

### 3. The characters compose back

A musician encounters a game world shaped by a song; a game character makes an artifact that later appears in a film or concert. The Full Measure world layer is a possible *experience carrier*, not owner of the story, the players, or donor systems.

The strange animal-rescue operation crosses the house's orbit through **particular encounters**. Do not reveal all animals to be monsters, make them programmable, or reduce _The Pet Sitter_ to a side quest. Deuce, Renji, the substitute, and the animals retain separate goals and partial views.

### 4. The telescope — relationships, not final answers

A telescope may offer views of different cuts of the same world: a saved stream, a book annotation, a route a rat remembers, an unplayed melody. It can reveal a useful relationship without making a narrator omniscient or naming one definitive interpretation.

National Treasure contributes the *puzzle-box posture*: an excellent clue gives the next question. ALEX, 3rdi, Dogram, LOADOUT, and MEMENTO may inspire different **creative disciplines** (evidence, visibility, relation, carry, and residue), but naming them here does not assert interoperability, implementation, or transferred authority.

### 5. The kitchen — the smallest stakes are real

The largest possible cross-media mystery can turn on a meal, a missing creature, shelter, a witness, a song someone needs to hear, or a route back to a companion.

Garden / NanaSpork / Upper Room / Jubilee / Book of Acts evoke a separate real-world concern: a proposal to help is not the same thing as someone receiving help. The fiction may honor this distinction without dramatizing actual private needs or pretending a story beat verifies a deployed service.

### 6. The audience — entry without capture

A film's credits may point toward an independent song; a song may suggest a game; a game may point toward a live event. Someone may arrive and contribute a **new, consensually attributed** artifact. The artifact can become a candidate for a later work if its creators choose. Attendance, watching, playing, or appearing near an event is never consent to make a real person a fictional character or declare their contribution canon.

The film must have its own ending. A listener must be able to enjoy the song without playing the game. A player must be able to understand their immediate choices without consuming the full mythology. **Cross-media entrances enrich one another; they do not hold each other hostage.**

## A scene, not a cosmology lecture

At the house, somebody points the telescope back at the house. For an instant it resembles a musical score. A cat leaves the room. An old recording finishes, yet an unheard song appears in the credits.

Cut to the kitchen. Somebody knocks. Somebody else says: **"Come in. We made too much."**

The scene's immediate outcome is ordinary human hospitality, not the resolution of every secret. Whether the musical-score view is literal in-world physics, a visual metaphor, a recording artifact, or a misreading is **unfixed**.

## First bounded production experiment — THE FIRST DOOR

**Proposed companion:** [THE FIRST DOOR — A Room Remembers Differently](the-first-door-novelist-memento-scene-seed.md), a bounded Novelist × MEMENTO-inspired scene seed that follows this charter without changing CANON 001 or admitting new story-world history.

Compose **one fictional occurrence and three independent presentations**, not a master lore dump:

1. **Occurrence:** a small, local need after the Collection Event, such as an animal needing a safe route or a person needing an ordinary meal. Name participants, what each could perceive, their choices, and one actual consequence.
2. **Song / audio artifact:** a short track or found-sound sketch derived from that occurrence. Label which sounds are witnessed within the fiction and which are invented as creative treatment.
3. **Scene / playable cut:** a 60–90 second house scene *or* tiny player-facing encounter showing a different limited view. An audience beginning here must understand the local emotional stakes without the song.
4. **Crossing receipt:** record the source occurrence; each carrier's author and perspective; what changed between carriers; what remained fixed; what is still unknown; any permission needed to use a real person's contribution.
5. **Hostile check:** let one carrier disagree, omit a detail, or arrive later. The story must remain intelligible **without** treating the discrepancy as a retcon, a secret master narrator, or a forced new canon.

**Pass:** each entrance stands independently; an attributable relationship survives between them; the audience can distinguish an established fictional event, an interpretation, and a fresh invention; a participant can decline to cross without losing the meaning of their original work.

**Failure:** a second medium invalidates the first; a viewer's interpretation rewrites the rat rescue; the house becomes a master system; a real collaborator is silently fictionalized; audience attendance is mistaken for ownership or consent.
