## 2026-08-10 — Vocabulary often arrives after recurrence

**Status:** Observation

**Source:** Cross-project Collective work.

**Note:** The same structural ideas often recur at several scales before anyone gives them a shared name. Concepts such as anchors, receipts, fields, ghosts, garments, and authority became easier to reason about once their recurring shape was named independently of any one implementation.

**Why keep it:** When a strange idea appears in one project, check whether it is actually a local instance of a pattern already appearing elsewhere. Naming the portable phenomenon can reduce architectural duplication.

**Promotion target:** Mature definitions belong in `glossary/`; stable laws belong in `patterns/`.

## 2026-08-10 — Weird metaphor can be a compression layer

**Status:** Hypothesis

**Source:** Repeated design conversations.

**Note:** A metaphor that initially sounds unserious can sometimes preserve the correct structural distinction better than formal vocabulary invented too early. “Garment” versus “atmosphere,” “ghost,” “frontier child,” and similar language became useful because the metaphor carried constraints with it.

**Why keep it:** Do not discard a strange phrase merely because it is strange. First ask whether it compresses a real invariant.

**Promotion target:** `glossary/` or `patterns/` if the invariant survives repeated specimens.
