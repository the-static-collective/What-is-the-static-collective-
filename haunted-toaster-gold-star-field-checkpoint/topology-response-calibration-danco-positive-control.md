# Topology Response Calibration — DANCO Positive Control

**Status:** field evidence / calibration control, not yet a portable law

**GitHub authority:** Haunted Toaster issue #113

## Why this specimen matters

`release (DANCO)` is the current gold positive control for **topology responsiveness**.

The packaged render was judged unusually alive at the topology layer itself, not merely energetic because of camera or surface effects. Its receipt shows a useful combination:

* source duration: about `143.4s`;
* renderer: `visual-language-v3`;
* base topology: `quad-mirror` / `quad-mirror-v2`;
* primitive field: `branches` + `wave`;
* response policy: `elastic-topology-response-v1`;
* nested response granularity: `phrase`;
* full bounded contour: **48 response knots**;
* section energy with substantial surviving local difference, approximately `.41 → .45 → .92 → .58 → .84 → .48`;
* topology apparitions into `echo-tunnel` and `cathedral-fan` while motion, camera, and material retain their own independent transitions.

The important observation is therefore not an aesthetic recipe such as “use quad-mirror for this kind of song.”

It is a response target:

> **Whatever topology you are, listen this hard.**

## Calibration hypothesis

Heavy or highly mastered material may contain plenty of nominal energy while losing enough **local surviving difference** before or during response preparation that topology receives too little phrase-scale information to breathe, recoil, swell, and change state.

That refines the earlier Compression Pressure problem.

Do **not** solve the heavy-song case by simply making it more intense.

Working experimental law:

> **Do not make heavy songs more intense. Preserve enough local musical difference that topology has information to respond to.**

This remains a hypothesis until contrasted against negative controls.

## Experiment

Use a small comparative fixture set:

1. **Positive control:** `release (DANCO)`.
2. **Negative control A:** one topologically-dead heavy specimen.
3. **Negative control B:** a second dead-heavy specimen if available.

For each specimen, compare the response evidence before topology execution:

* source dynamic spread where available;
* section and phrase-local deltas;
* number and distribution of source response knots;
* compaction into the bounded contour;
* peak/headroom saturation;
* inferred transient and low-end event salience where available;
* topology excursion/apparition evidence after response compilation.

Primary question:

> **What response evidence survives into DANCO's 48 phrase-level knots that collapses or becomes visually inert in the heavy negative controls?**

The result should tell us whether the missing information is lost in source analysis, response normalization, compaction, saturation/headroom, or topology consumption.

## Guardrails

* Do not tune the visual aesthetic toward DANCO's quad-mirror appearance.
* Do not globally lower or raise renderer intensity from one specimen.
* Keep camera response independent; camera excitement is not proof that topology listened.
* Preserve silence and real hush.
* Preserve peak headroom.
* Treat inferred control lanes as analysis evidence, never literal recovered stems.
* Promote this into a portable Creative Field law only after the positive/negative comparison survives multiple specimens.

## Relationship to existing evidence

This control complements rather than replaces the earlier checkpoints:

* **Crooked Handle** shows that spacious internal contrast already produces strong visual articulation.
* **Air Moved** suggests dense material can spend motion headroom too early and remain active but dynamically flat.
* **DANCO** supplies a concrete target for what successful phrase-scale topology response looks like.

Together they turn “heavy songs look flat” from a vague complaint into a falsifiable response-calibration experiment.
