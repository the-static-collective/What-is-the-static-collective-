# Not-Pastor Ron — Meet the Story in Public

**Status:** active seed / first encounter pending

**GitHub seed:** https://github.com/the-static-collective/What-is-the-static-collective-/issues/9

## Attractor

> **What happens when a man who learned some of God's ways the hard way finally meets God's Story?**

Ron sits at an unusual crossing:

- pastor / shepherd;
- teacher;
- recovery and drug counselor;
- self-described master fuck-up;
- self-formed believer;
- non-conformist;
- someone who knows the Creator relationally but does not yet know His Story deeply.

The apparent contradiction is not a branding problem to solve. **It is the project.**

The first responsibility is therefore not to turn Ron into a polished Bible teacher. It is to preserve the actual starting condition long enough for something real to happen.

## Working identity

**Not-Pastor Ron**

Working promise:

> **A man who knows the Creator, finally learning His story.**

"Not-Pastor" does not mean anti-pastor. It names a refusal of costume, pedestal, and performed certainty.

Ron may shepherd. Ron may teach. Ron may have genuine spiritual knowledge. None of those require him to pretend he already knows the terrain he is entering.

## Core law

> **Ron does not preach from above the text. He meets it in public.**

This protects the organism from collapsing into ordinary religious content.

His unfamiliarity with the Biblical Story is not merely a deficiency that must be repaired before he is allowed to speak. It is part of the initial evidence.

The audience is permitted to witness recognition, confusion, argument, surprise, correction, laughter, discomfort, and changed understanding.

## Formation inversion

A conventional formation path often looks roughly like:

`learn the Story → derive beliefs → attempt to live them`

Ron appears to have traveled substantially in the other direction:

`live → fail → encounter Creator → form beliefs → discover the ancient Story`

That inversion gives the project its investigative force.

Ron can now walk backward through his own formation and ask:

- Where did lived experience independently converge with the Story?
- Where did it not?
- Which beliefs were actually inherited from church culture rather than discovered?
- Which patterns from recovery, teaching, counseling, failure, surrender, and restoration suddenly become legible inside the text?
- Where does the text refuse Ron's existing model?

The project is not valuable because Ron is automatically right. It is valuable because the meeting can remain visible.

## Encounter grammar v0

Use one bounded narrative encounter at a time.

### 1. Story

Choose one Biblical episode with enough surrounding context to preserve its narrative integrity.

Do not begin with isolated proof-texts or a doctrinal conclusion Ron is expected to reach.

### 2. Ron

Capture the first encounter before explanation smooths it out.

Useful material includes:

- what he notices immediately;
- what he misses;
- what confuses him;
- what irritates him;
- what makes him laugh;
- what feels familiar;
- what contradicts what he thought Christians believed;
- what questions he actually asks.

### 3. Life

Then allow Ron's existing human instruments to enter.

The counselor may recognize dependency, denial, substitution, relapse, surrender, control, repetition, restoration, and consequence.

The teacher may see structure and sequence.

The pastor may see the person inside the doctrine.

The man who has failed may recognize people he has been.

The non-conformist may refuse a conventional explanation when the story itself does not appear to support it.

These are lenses, not automatic authority.

### 4. Return

Return to the text after the first reaction and lived interpretation.

Ask:

- What changed on the second pass?
- What did Ron initially misread?
- What did he see that deserves another look?
- What remains unresolved?
- What neighboring story has now become relevant?

Every encounter should be allowed to end without false closure.

## Lawful outputs

These are successful outputs, not failures of production:

- “I don't know.”
- “I was wrong about this.”
- “This bothers me.”
- “I recognize this person because I have been him.”
- “I don't think I understood this the first time.”
- “People say this means X, but I'm not sure this story actually says X.”
- unresolved disagreement;
- a question strong enough to become the next encounter.

The project should never manufacture certainty merely to make an episode feel finished.

## Audience is downstream

Do not define the project first as content for addicts, men's ministry, deconstruction, or Bible education.

Those categories may eventually describe people who find the work useful, but they should not determine Ron's voice before the organism has shown itself.

Likely resonance may emerge among people who believe in God but distrust religious machinery, people with recovery experience, people who did not grow up knowing Scripture, and people who have been handed conclusions without being shown the strange whole Story.

That is an inference, not yet a target-market claim.

## Do not choose the container yet

Possible future containers include:

- video;
- podcast;
- written field notes;
- book;
- recovery-circle material;
- classroom material;
- live conversations;
- pastoral material.

Do not let any one of these become the project before the first encounter exists.

**The organism comes before the container.**

## First field test

Record one real encounter now.

Minimum specimen:

1. Choose one narratively complete Biblical story.
2. Ron reads or listens without preparing a sermon.
3. Preserve the first reaction separately from later interpretation.
4. Let the conversation move naturally through Story → Ron → Life → Return.
5. End with three explicit fields:
   - **new illumination**;
   - **residual fog**;
   - **next door**.

The test succeeds if Ron becomes more recognizably Ron while encountering Scripture.

It does **not** succeed merely because Ron sounds more like a conventional Bible teacher at the end.

## Minimal encounter receipt

For now, preserve only enough structure to remember what actually happened:

```text
Encounter:
Story entered:
Starting assumption:
First reaction:
Lived pattern recognized:
Textual return:

Residual fog:
Next door:

```

This is a witness record, not a grading rubric.

## Graduation condition

The seed should graduate into a dedicated `not-pastor-ron` repository after at least one real encounter has happened and we can name which parts of this grammar survived contact with Ron.

At that point, project-owned evidence should outrank this incubator page.

Until then:

- **GitHub issue #9** is the executable seed;
- this page is the narrative witness;
- Ron himself remains the primary human authority over whether the project actually resembles him.

## Residual fog

Still intentionally unresolved:

- the first Biblical story;
- whether the natural medium is video, audio, text, or mixed;
- whether another person should be present as interlocutor;
- how much contextual scholarship should enter during the first encounter versus afterward;
- whether Ron wants his recovery-counseling identity foregrounded publicly;
- whether the eventual public identity keeps the exact name **Not-Pastor Ron** after field contact.

Do not resolve these from the armchair when the first encounter can teach us more cheaply.
