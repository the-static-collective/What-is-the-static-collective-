## The textual claim is stronger than “Moses received commandments”

Within the Torah's own narrative world, the first tablets are described as:

```
SUBSTRATE = work of God
WRITING   = writing of God
MEDIUM    = stone
AUTHOR    = God / “finger of God”
```

Exodus 31:18 says the tablets were written “with the finger of God.” Exodus 32:15–16 says the tablets themselves were the work of God and “the writing was the writing of God, engraved on the tablets.” Deuteronomy 9:10 repeats that they were written with the finger of God and contained the words spoken at the mountain.

The Decalogue text itself contains the divine name `YHWH` repeatedly. In particular:

```
“I am YHWH your God ...”

and

“You shall not take the NAME of YHWH your God ...”
```

So, **inside the literary claim**, God is not merely said to reveal the Name orally. God is represented as authoring an artifact whose writing includes God's own Name.

Candidate compression:

> **THE NAME IS SELF-INSCRIBED.**

That is a textual/narrative proposition, not yet an archaeological one.

## The evidence cliff — what did the glyphs look like?

This is where the gold-star note must become strict.

We **do not possess the tablets** and no archaeological artifact lets us recover their letterforms. The biblical manuscripts that preserve Exodus and Deuteronomy are later textual witnesses, not photographs or graphic tracings of Sinai.

Therefore:

```
BIBLICAL NARRATIVE CLAIM:
God wrote the words / Name on stone.

DOES NOT ENTAIL:
we know the original glyph shapes.
```

Early alphabetic writing existed in Egypt/Sinai and the southern Levant well before the Iron Age. Proto-Sinaitic / proto-alphabetic inscriptions from Serabit el-Khadim and related finds demonstrate that West Semitic alphabetic writing was possible in the broad cultural world in which later Exodus traditions are situated. But that does not identify the script of the tablets.

Likewise, later Paleo-Hebrew / Phoenician-family forms are genuine historical scripts for Israel/Judah, but **projecting those familiar glyphs backward onto the tablets is reconstruction, not observation**.

Historical scholarship is even more cautious because the secure nonbiblical attestation of `YHWH` as the Israelite deity is much later than any traditional date for Moses; proposals for Bronze Age attestations remain disputed.

So preserve:

> **There is a lost graphic object at the center of the narrative.**

Not:

> “We have recovered God's handwriting.”
