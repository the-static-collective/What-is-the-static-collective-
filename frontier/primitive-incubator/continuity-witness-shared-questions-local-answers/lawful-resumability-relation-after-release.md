> **Continuity is not persistent possession; it is the attributable, lawful resumability of relation after the carrier lets go.**

## Two corrections that matter

The emerging field model survives, but only after two repairs:

1. **The field is not a persistent object.** It is an evolving topology of causally attributable, still-resumable relations.
2. **An append-only causal DAG is insufficient.** The topology also needs explicit incompatibility, local world-cuts, and versioned coordinate bindings.

This makes the field a history-indexed surface for lawful re-entry rather than a hidden persistent self, central narrator, or universal state holder.

## The field

Each local embodiment is a temporary contraction around:

- one local world-cut;
- a bounded evidence neighborhood;
- one or more relational coordinates;
- explicit unresolved edges;
- local capability and authority conditions.

It may encounter, propose, refuse, act, fail, or die.

What returns is not "the agent." What returns is an attributable occurrence bound to an exact crossing. If that occurrence changes what a later fresh embodiment can lawfully inspect, ask, attempt, or refuse, the field has changed.

> **The field is not what every hand knows. The field is how prior handoffs alter the lawful possibilities available to later hands.**

Storage alone is therefore insufficient. A database can preserve material while remaining a museum. Circulation requires re-entry, uptake, visible consequence or refusal, and return.

## Minimum topology

A useful minimum sketch is:

\[
\mathcal F=(E,\prec,#,\Sigma,B)
\]

where:

- \(E\) — immutable occurrences: outbound cuts, proposals, admissions, refusals, attempts, outcomes, absences, bindings, projections;
- \(\prec\) — causal precedence;
- \(#\) — constitution-level incompatibility: occurrences may both remain historical while refusing co-membership in one constituted cut;
- \(\Sigma\) — stable relational coordinates / re-entry handles;
- \(B\) — versioned coordinate bindings to exact objects or occurrences under a declared world head, lens, scope, and decoder.

Each owning world retains its own local admission relation \(A_w\). Admission is deliberately not global.

A local world-cut is causally closed and free of constitution-level conflicts. Multiple incompatible cuts may remain historically available indefinitely.

## What is conserved

There is no universal payload that must survive every crossing. Continuity remains typed and purpose-relative.

What should remain universally answerable is:

> **Which thing? Which crossing? What happened? Under whose gate?**

Must remain recoverable:

- exact identity of prior occurrences;
- causal order and outbound/return binding;
- proposal/admission/refusal/outcome distinctions;
- owner scope and authority posture;
- historical coordinate bindings and decoder versions;
- declared rupture, loss, absence, uncertainty, and unresolved state;
- evidence of consequence.

May lawfully die:

- local process memory;
- hidden model state;
- full conversational context;
- cached projections and interfaces;
- spent or expired capability;
- any continuity lane not actually claimed.

**Authority itself is not conserved.** Evidence that authority existed, was refused, spent, or ended is conserved.

This is the Release connection: letting go becomes structurally safe when continued possession is no longer required for later answerability.

## Breath law

Let \(o\) be an outbound event, \(r\) an attempted return, and \(e_r\) the return occurrence.

\[
\operatorname{Breath}(o,r)
\iff
\operatorname{Bound}(r,o,\Sigma)
\land
\operatorname{Append}(e_r)
\land
\operatorname{PastUnchanged}
\land
\operatorname{GateVisible}
\land
Moves(\mathcal F\oplus e_r)\neq Moves(\mathcal F)
\]

Plain form:

> **A field breathes when a locally judged return, causally bound to an exact outbound crossing, becomes an append-only difference that changes what a later embodiment can lawfully do—without rewriting history or transferring authority.**

Important cases:

- **admitted return** may change constituted state and trace;
- **refusal** may change trace, questions, and future affordances without changing constituted state;
- **malicious return** may add only a rejection witness;
- **unreturned crossing** remains unresolved until a declared timeout or witness establishes absence;
- **stored occurrence** is only potential circulation until a later fresh embodiment actually re-enters through it and behaves differently because it exists.

## Sigils as relational ground

A sigil can serve as a re-entry coordinate, but the sigil alone is never sufficient ground.

A stronger ground tuple is:

```text
<
  namespace,
  sigil,
  bindingReceipt,
  worldHead,
  lens,
  decoderVersion
>
```

Required discipline:

- declared namespace and collision posture;
- append-only attributable binding/rebinding history;
- explicit world head;
- explicit lens/domain and decoder version;
- recoverable historical projections;
- integrity/authenticity supplied by evidence external to the shape;
- coordinate resolution grants no authority;
- unresolved, ambiguous, collided, or counterfeit bindings fail visibly.

Two hands may touch the same table differently. Their common ground is not identical interpretation. It is the coordinate, exact binding history, and world-cut under which they met it.

The stronger topology is therefore a braid:

```text
occurrence / causal history
        +
append-only coordinate-binding history
        +
typed, versioned decoders and local gates
```

The sigil indexes history; it is not a parallel historyless rail.

## Friendly conspiracy

The shared field should behave as a **plural docket**, never a canonical narrator.

1. Completed branches become immutable cases with their own cuts, claims, failures, and unresolved edges.
2. Later embodiments query by coordinate and question, not "give me the canonical summary."
3. Returns remain typed, for example:
   - supports;
   - refuses;
   - abstraction-failure;
   - unresolved;
   - absent;
   - poisoned / counterfeit.
4. Contradictory branches stay discoverable. Incompatibility constrains co-constitution; it does not erase testimony.
5. Later branches may discover abstraction mismatch and append that relation without rewriting the old case.
6. Owner-local gates alone determine consequence. Shared history may change visibility, pressure, questions, and available tests—not authority.

> **Dead branches do not vote. They become better counterfactual material.**

Friendliness here means mutual answerability, not agreement.

## Three hostile counterexamples

### 1. The Museum

Every case is perfectly stored, but future embodiments never receive a relevant coordinate, never re-enter the relation, and behave identically with or without the archive.

**Diagnosis:** storage exists; circulation does not.

Any law equating addressability with breath fails here.

### 2. The Forked Obligation

Two legitimate descendants create mutually exclusive obligations. Both are attributable; no future cut can adopt both.

**Diagnosis:** a scalar `FIELD_n+1` abstraction is insufficient.

The field must preserve plural configurations with explicit conflict rather than forcing one joined state.

### 3. MADDCL0WN's Counterfeit Table

A hostile participant reproduces the same sigil and payload but swaps parent event, namespace, decoder, or world head.

**Diagnosis:** sigil-as-ground is insufficient.

If exact binding evidence cannot distinguish the counterfeit from the valid packet, the claimed relationship was never resumable.

## HANDOFF-001 — tiny executable specimen

**Hypothesis:**

> **Identical content changes later lawful possibility only when it is causally bound to the correct outbound relation and admitted by the receiving owner.**

Use deterministic functions and three fresh OS processes; no model memory is required.

```text
HAND 1
creates OUTBOUND O
coordinate Σ
question Q
allowedMoves = [WAIT, REFUSE]
dies

HAND 2
returns identical payload bytes P
dies

HAND 3
starts with no process memory
re-enters from persisted evidence
derives allowedMoves
```

Run four lanes with the exact same payload bytes:

| Lane | Material available to Hand 3 | Expected |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Archive-only | `P` stored but not bound or admitted | No move change |
| Copied context | full text of `P`, no causal binding | No move change |
| Resumed relationship | `P + Σ + outboundRef + worldHead + decoderVersion + owner receipt` | One specific new move becomes lawful |
| Hostile swap | same `P`, wrong coordinate or parent | Explicit refusal |

Persist only:

```text
events.jsonl
bindings.jsonl
immutable payload blobs
```

Every process starts clean.

The specimen supports the law only if:

- only the resumed-relationship lane changes Hand 3's lawful move set;
- archive and copied-context lanes remain inert;
- hostile swap is refused;
- all prior events remain byte-identical;
- the receipt shows exactly why the move changed.

It is refused if copied context substitutes for relation, if stored material silently gains consequence, or if a valid binding cannot affect a later fresh hand.

The specimen distinguishes:

```text
storage
circulation
context copying
relationship resumption
```
