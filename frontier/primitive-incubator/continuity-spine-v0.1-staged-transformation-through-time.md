# Continuity Spine v0.1 — Staged Transformation Through Time

**Status:** first TranchNode specimen landed / project-backed hypothesis retained

**Canonical authority:** TranchNode issue #56, design/plan, and landed PR #58

## The distinction

A project snapshot describes what is true now. A roadmap describes intended work. **Continuity Spine** describes the lawful relationship between what a project was, what it is, and what it proposes to become without letting the proposed future impersonate constituted reality.

Working compression:

> **Do not design only the finished system. Design the sequence of systems capable of becoming it.**

The executable v0.1 carries four laws:

1. **Future constrains proposal, never evidence.**
2. **Overlap before replacement.**
3. **Transfer before shedding.**
4. **Witness before claiming transfer complete.**

Operationally:

```
inherit -> grow -> overlap -> transfer -> witness -> shed -> continue
```

## Why the overlap matters

The transformation shape is not merely `A -> B`.

```
A
  -> grow B
A + B
  -> transfer responsibility
A* + B
  -> witness transfer
B
  -> shed obsolete scaffold
```

The `A + B` state is constitutive. The old carrier remains until the new carrier can actually bear the dependency or responsibility. After witnessed transfer, obsolete scaffolding may be shed without confusing dead weight with continuity.

## First executable specimen — Intent Stroke v0.1 -> v0.2

TranchNode PR #58 pins the already-landed Intent Stroke transition from PR #54 as the first calibration specimen.

The v0.2 wrapper added a new path while preserving v0.1. During the overlap:

* v0.1 remained supported;
* v0.2 accepted raw points plus declared layout;
* TranchNode bound the canonical layout internally;
* the canonical decoder remained unchanged;
* decoder authority remained `none`;
* exact collisions remained unresolved;
* raw pointer transport gained no traversal authority.

The transferred responsibility is **canonical layout binding for v0.2 callers**. Once the declared PR #54 witnesses are supplied, the evaluator permits the caller-side `fieldLayoutRef` construction dependency to be shed. The old v0.1 interface is not deleted because compatibility is an independent invariant still being carried.

## What v0.1 can now refuse

The pure evaluator can deterministically distinguish and refuse:

* an attractor claiming constituted/evidence status;
* backward or structurally invalid stage order;
* loss of an active invariant;
* a carried responsibility disappearing without a transfer;
* a declared transfer claimed complete without its required witnesses;
* shedding before a relevant transfer is witnessed;
* an unknown witness label being smuggled in as evidence.

A proposal destination may still be structurally admissible, but the result remains explicitly `proposal_only`. Evaluation does not constitute the future.

## What this does not prove

This specimen does **not** provide:

* autonomous project planning;
* task generation or scheduling;
* CI/CD orchestration;
* automatic deletion of code, dependencies, or infrastructure;
* prediction of the correct future;
* generalized temporal logic;
* cross-repository mutation;
* universal Continuity Spine schema authority;
* authentication of evidence beyond the project-native references supplied to the evaluator.

The evaluator describes and refuses transformations. It does not execute them.

## Relationship to Lawful Reachability

[Lawful Reachability / Constituted Reality](lawful-reachability-constituted-reality.md) asks what belongs to the present constituted world given accountable causal history.

Continuity Spine adds a prospective relation among successive cuts without weakening that boundary:

> **The future may constrain proposals backward; it may not grant evidence backward.**

A proposed attractor can tell the present what capabilities will eventually be required or what dependency cannot remain forever. Only admitted events and witnesses can turn a proposed transition into history.

## Relationship to BEE

[BEE Protocol — Boundary-Carried Invariants](../../bee-protocol-boundary-carried-invariants/) concerns an invariant crossing from one project to another while donor authority stays behind and the recipient proves it locally.

Continuity Spine concerns **responsibility and dependency crossing from one stage to another through time**. The two are compatible but not identical.

A later cross-project transplant may use BEE to carry the Continuity Spine invariant, but this first TranchNode specimen does not grant its schema authority to other repositories.

## Graduation boundary

Do **not** promote Continuity Spine to a shared Pattern from one TranchNode specimen.

A materially different second system should reproduce the useful invariant in its own native contract before the Collective claims a cross-project law. That second specimen should be allowed to disagree with TranchNode's local schema while testing the deeper invariant:

> **Build the next load path before weakening the old one; shed the old load path only after its required function has crossed and the transfer has been witnessed.**
