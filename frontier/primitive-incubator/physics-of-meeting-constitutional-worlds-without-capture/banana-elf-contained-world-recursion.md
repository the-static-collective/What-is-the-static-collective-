# Banana Elf — Contained World Recursion

> **Status:** tiny frontier specimen / inslice.
>
> This note does not claim that an imagined inhabitant has independent outer-world existence. It records a narrower causal fact: a represented world inside a participant can alter the world that contains that participant.

## The loop

```text
WORLD
  contains person
    contains thought
      contains world
        contains inhabitant
          contains behavior
            leaves residue
              changes person
                changes WORLD
```

The contained world does not need to be identical to the containing world.

It may be fictional, remembered, compressed, hypothetical, symbolic, dreamed, simulated, or mistaken. The important distinction is between **existence in a frame**, **authority in a frame**, and **consequence across frames**.

A contained inhabitant can lack sovereign standing in the outer world while still participating in an outer-world causal chain through the person who contains the representation.

## Banana-elf specimen

```text
outer world
  contains Lu
    contains banana thought
      contains banana world
        contains banana elf
          does banana-elf things
            causes laughter / recognition
              changes conversation
                leaves durable trace
                  changes outer world'
```

The prime mark matters:

> **The world that contained the thought may not be the same world after the thought has acted through its container.**

The banana elf is therefore fictional in one frame and causally consequential across another.

That is not a contradiction. It is a frame distinction.

## Candidate distinction

```text
existence != authority != consequence
```

Something need not possess outer-world authority, or even outer-world ontological independence, to produce a consequence in the outer world.

A map can change the territory because a participant reads the map and acts.

A story can change a law because a participant understands the story and chooses differently.

An imagined creature can change a conversation because it makes someone laugh, notice a pattern, name a structure, or preserve a memory.

## Relation to Constitutional Biology

Constitutional Biology asks whether distinct local worlds and participants can exchange attributable influence without collapsing their boundaries.

Contained-world recursion adds a smaller nested case:

```text
containing world
      ↓ contains
participant
      ↓ contains
represented world
      ↓ generates
local consequence
      ↓ crosses through participant
changed containing world
```

The crossing point is the participant.

The representation does not silently acquire authority over the containing world. Consequence becomes outer-world consequence only through some admitted act, judgment, expression, memory, or behavior of the containing participant.

This suggests a useful membrane rule:

> **Represented worlds may influence containing worlds through their hosts without thereby becoming sovereign in the containing frame.**

## Why preserve this tiny specimen

The banana elf is useful precisely because it is ridiculous enough to prevent the abstraction from hiding its mechanics.

The sequence is observable:

```text
thought
  → inner world
  → inhabitant
  → behavior
  → reaction
  → utterance
  → durable artifact
```

Nothing requires the inner inhabitant to be promoted into outer-world fact.

Yet the outer world has changed.

That makes the specimen relevant to fiction, simulation, memory, models, dreams, software worlds, narrative interfaces, and any architecture where local representations can produce lawful downstream consequence without receiving ambient authority.

For now, keep it small:

> **A world may contain a thought that contains a world whose inhabitants can help change the world that contains the thought.**
