# Static Collective Vocabulary

Shared terms that recur across projects. These definitions are intentionally portable: individual projects may refine them, but should not silently replace their meaning.

## Anchor

A deliberately preserved point of high confidence or human commitment that later inference, mutation, or recovery should route around rather than casually overwrite.

An anchor narrows lawful freedom without necessarily determining the whole result.

## Ancestor

An earlier form, preset, specimen, or proposal that contributes lineage to later work without necessarily remaining a mandatory choice or exhaustive category.

Ancestors are provenance, not prisons.

## Atmosphere

A phenomenon that moves through, around, or across a creative world: smoke, rain, dust, sparks, insects, stars, debris, and related fields.

Atmosphere answers **what is moving through the world?** It is distinct from garment, which answers what the world appears to be made of.

## Authority

The right or power to make a state-changing decision count as canonical within a defined scope.

Evidence, influence, confidence, visibility, and authorship do not automatically imply authority. Good systems make the boundary explicit.

## Compost

Material that is no longer treated as a finished artifact but remains fertile for future transformation.

Compost may include discarded fragments, failed attempts, rejected directions, residue, or obsolete forms. Its value lies in recoverable possibility, not in pretending it is still canonical.

## Field

A shared space of possible states, signals, candidates, tensions, or influences in which no single element necessarily defines the whole.

A field is often the right abstraction when useful behavior emerges from relationships among many partial contributions rather than one central object.

A field may also describe a bounded state of awareness: the evidence, salience, tensions, unresolved questions, relationships, and frontier presently visible from a particular position. Shared underlying evidence does not require every observer to inhabit an identical field.

## Field snapshot

A bounded, addressable record of a field at a particular time or causal cut.

A field snapshot may preserve anchors, salient evidence, tensions, unresolved questions, supporting artifacts, visibility state, and frontier without claiming to capture an observer's complete consciousness.

## Frontier

The currently meaningful edge of explored possibility: the set or region from which the next genuinely new move can extend the work.

A frontier is not merely the latest item chronologically. It represents live creative or architectural possibility.

## Garment

A creative layer describing the apparent substance, surface, or material constitution of a world.

Garment answers **what is this world made of?** Examples may include photocopy, paper, grain, metal, ink, glass, or other material grammars. It should remain composable with independent axes such as atmosphere and motion.

## Ghost

A residual presence that remains after the source, participant, state, or event that produced it is no longer directly present.

A ghost can witness prior presence, preserve causality, or affect expression. It does not inherit the authority of its source merely by persisting.

## Illumination

A change in visibility produced when evidence, traversal, comparison, or encounter makes previously unnoticed structure inspectable.

Illumination may reveal a relation, contradiction, neighboring field, or frontier. It does not by itself make the revealed claim canonical or transfer authority to the illuminating process.

## Possession

A temporary condition in which one influence, grammar, participant, or state exerts unusually strong control over an otherwise broader system.

Possession should be attributable, bounded, and distinguishable from permanent ownership or hidden authority.

## Proposal

A candidate statement, artifact, mutation, placement, or state transition offered for consideration without being canonical merely because it exists.

Proposal preserves the distinction between **can be considered** and **has been accepted**.

## Receipt

Inspectable evidence that supports a claim about what happened, where something came from, or how a result was produced.

A receipt should make later verification easier. It is evidence of an event or derivation, not a substitute for the event itself.

## Resolved timeline

A temporal projection in which the system has converted sufficient evidence and decisions into an ordered, usable sequence while preserving unresolved material honestly rather than inventing timing to create superficial completeness.

Resolution describes the state of the projection, not omniscience about the source.

## Traversal

A meaningful transition from one field or bounded state of awareness to another.

A traversal should preserve enough provenance to explain what prompted the move, what became newly visible, what remained unresolved, and which kind of relation connected the fields. Traversal may illuminate a claim without accepting it as truth.

## Visibility

The observer- and time-relative degree to which a field, relation, tension, or frontier is presently inspectable.

Visibility should remain distinct from truth and authority. Useful systems may distinguish strongly evidenced territory from dim, fogged, contested, or presently unreachable territory without treating the map as complete.

## Witness

A participant, process, or artifact that records, attests to, or preserves evidence of what occurred without necessarily possessing authority to decide what should occur.

Witness and authority may coexist in one actor, but they are separate roles.

---

## Working rule

When a project gives one of these terms a narrower technical meaning, document the local refinement explicitly. Shared vocabulary should create semantic continuity across the Collective, not hidden coupling between implementations.
